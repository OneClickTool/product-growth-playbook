# 🔎 Research

Find out what users need, where they talk, and what's true before you build, launch or price anything.
Then keep your research in tidy Markdown files that you, your team and your AI agent can all use.

| Skill | What you get | Level | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| [research-source-finding](research-source-finding/SKILL.md) | 5–10 trusted sources per question, ranked, dated and cited | Beginner | 30–45 min |
| [research-doc-organization](research-doc-organization/SKILL.md) | A `research/` folder: index, sources, decision log, note template | Beginner | 30 min |
| [research-where-users-ask](research-where-users-ask/SKILL.md) | A map of the places people ask for software, with links, search queries and alerts | Beginner | 45–60 min |
| [research-review-mining](research-review-mining/SKILL.md) | Competitor pain points, opportunities and user phrases from 100+ reviews | Beginner | 60 min |

**Next step:** turn research into lessons with [Case Study](../case-study/) skills, e.g. a competitor teardown.

**Suggested order:** organize your folder → where users ask → review mining → source finding for each open question.

> 📖 Tip: a research folder is just Markdown files. Open it in [Markdown Viewer](https://oneclicktool.app/desktop/markdown-viewer?utm_source=github&utm_medium=area-readme&utm_campaign=growth-playbook&utm_content=research)
> (free, Mac, made by the maintainer) to see notes as a table, a link map or Finder-style folders, with ★ favorites.
