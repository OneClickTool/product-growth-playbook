# Shipfolks × GoViralDev: a small directory and a $150 sponsor slot

> Seen on 2026-10-09. Sources: [shipfolks.com](https://shipfolks.com/), [shipfolks.com/advertise](https://shipfolks.com/advertise),
> [goviraldev.com](https://goviraldev.com/?ref=shipfolks) and the page's HTML. Prices and counts are what the pages showed that day.

Two sides of one launch channel:

- **The directory (Shipfolks)**: how a brand-new directory looks full, earns trust and sells ads from day one.
- **The advertiser (GoViralDev)**: how a product uses a cheap, fixed-price sponsor slot to get in front of builders.

Most launch advice tells you to *submit* to directories. This case shows the other two moves: **buy a slot** on a small,
well-targeted one, or **run** one yourself.

## 1. What we saw

### Shipfolks, the directory
| What | Detail (2026-10-09) |
|---|---|
| Positioning | "Ship. Get found. Get hired." Products **and** the people who built them (freelancer profiles, "open to work") |
| Size | 29 projects, 60 builders, 57 open to work, 28 categories |
| Listing | Free. A name, a link and a screenshot. "A person reads every one before it goes up." The order "is not for sale" |
| Seeding | Most listings are well-known tools (Supabase, Raycast, Railway, Cursor, Fly.io) marked *listed by Shipfolks*. The directory filled itself with recognisable names before makers arrived |
| Ranking | Sort by *Newest* or *Most opened*; a "Most opened — 14 days" box shows a count of people per product (Supabase 60, Raycast 53, Railway 49, GoViralDev 49) |
| Outbound links | Every *Visit* link adds `?ref=shipfolks`, so the product owner sees Shipfolks in their analytics. Listing links are `rel="nofollow"`, ad links `rel="sponsored nofollow"` (no SEO backlink, and it says so in the markup) |
| Ads | **Two slots, one advertiser each:** in-feed banner between the 4th and 5th project, 1200×240, **$150 / 30 days**; sidebar card under Categories, 640×400, **$100 / 30 days**. Shown on the front page and every category page |
| Ad terms | Pay → slot comes off sale → email with size and link format → upload art. The 30 days start when the ad goes live. One image, one link, no tracking scripts. No adult or crypto trading. Buying an ad doesn't buy a position in the list. Prices are "introductory" |
| Retention | Freelancer profiles, jobs, blog and tools pages give builders a reason to come back, not just makers to submit once |

### GoViralDev, the advertiser
| What | Detail (2026-10-09) |

## 2. Why it works

**For the directory**
1. **Look full on day one.** Seeding with famous tools makes a 29-item site feel like a real index, and those listings
   still send useful clicks. It's honest because each one says *listed by Shipfolks*.
2. **Trust is the product.** Free listing, human review, "order not for sale", nofollow labelled correctly. Advertisers
   pay because readers trust the list, so the rules protect the ad revenue.
3. **Tiny, fixed ad inventory.** Two slots, one advertiser each, flat price, 30 days from go-live. No auction, no
   dashboard, no sales calls. A solo maker can sell and run it from a single page.
4. **`?ref=` proves the value without a tracking pixel.** The advertiser sees Shipfolks in their own analytics, so
   renewals sell themselves (or don't) with the advertiser's own data.
5. **Two audiences, one site.** Products bring makers; freelancer profiles and jobs bring people who come back.

**For the advertiser**
1. **Cheap, capped, measurable.** $150 is less than most one-day ad tests, the cost is fixed, and `?ref=shipfolks`
   shows up in analytics without any setup.
2. **The audience matches the offer.** A directory of builders, shown a tool for builders who need to market what they
   ship.
3. **The banner explains the product in one look.** A pipeline of boxes (input → steps → where it gets posted) beats a
   screenshot at 1200×240.
4. **Lead with the difference.** "Self-hosted, one-time, no subscription" stands out among AI tools that all charge
   monthly credits.
5. **Ad + listing + Most opened.** The ad drives opens; opens put the product in the *Most opened* box; the box gives a
   second free placement.

## 3. What to avoid (lessons from the gaps)
- **Ad and landing say different things.** The ad sells "go viral on autopilot, self-host, one-time". The landing opens
  with "All In One Platform" and monthly plans; the one-time offer is further down. Send ad traffic to a page (or anchor)
  whose first screen repeats the ad's promise.
- **No proof on the landing.** Paid clicks from a cold audience need one example video, one number or one user quote
  above the fold.
- **Expecting SEO.** Directory links like these are nofollow / sponsored. Buy them for clicks, not for rankings.
- **Not tagging the link yourself.** `?ref=` is the directory's tag. Add your own UTM too, so a 30-day test can be
  compared with other channels in one report.
- **Judging by opens.** 49 opens in 14 days is attention, not revenue. Decide your stop rule on sign-ups or sales.

## 4. Run it yourself

### A. Buy a sponsor slot (advertiser)
1. **Find 5 small directories your buyers browse** (builders, Mac users, designers…). Good signs: human review, a
   public flat price, few slots, a category that matches you, outbound `?ref=` links.
2. **Check they're alive.** Recent listings this month? Real makers, not only seeded big brands? Ask for numbers
   (visits, clicks to sponsors). No numbers → the test is the measurement; keep it at one month.
3. **Set the stop rule before paying.** Example: renew only if the slot brings ≥ N sign-ups or ≥ 1 sale in 30 days,
   where N comes from your price and conversion rate ([Spend and Timing](../skills/strategy/strategy-spend-and-timing/SKILL.md)).
4. **Make one banner per size.** Feed (wide): your pipeline or before → after in one line + the one difference.
   Sidebar (card): headline, one line, one button.
5. **Link to a matching page:** first screen repeats the ad headline, has one proof and one CTA. Add UTMs:
   `?ref=<directory>&utm_source=<directory>&utm_medium=sponsor&utm_campaign=<month>`.
6. **Also take the free listing** on the same site, so you're there after the ad ends.
7. **Day 30:** clicks → sign-ups → sales by source. Renew, move to another directory, or stop.

### B. Run a small directory (owner), if your audience is a niche you already serve
1. Pick a niche you can review with your own eyes (e.g. "Mac utilities", "tools for indie game devs").
2. Seed 20–30 useful, well-known entries labelled *listed by <you>*, so it's useful on day one.
3. Free listing, human review, publish the rules ("order not for sale", nofollow / sponsored).
4. Add `?ref=<you>` to every outbound link.
5. Open **one or two** flat-price slots with an `/advertise` page: size, price, 30 days from go-live, what's not
   accepted, how to book.
6. Give visitors a reason to come back (people profiles, jobs, a weekly "new this week" list).
7. Put your own products in the slot when it's unsold; it's still a place your users see.

## 5. Checklist

- [ ] 5 directories found; each one checked for recent listings, flat price, and `?ref=` on links
- [ ] Stop rule written down before paying
- [ ] Banner in both sizes; headline = the one difference
- [ ] Landing first screen repeats the ad, with one proof
- [ ] UTMs on the ad link + free listing on the same site
- [ ] Day-30 numbers by source, then decide: renew / move / stop
