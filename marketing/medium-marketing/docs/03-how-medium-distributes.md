# 03 · How Medium decides who sees your story, in plain words

Sources for every line: [reference/medium-facts.md](../reference/medium-facts.md#distribution-and-boost).

## Three levels

| Level | Who sees it | How you get there |
|---|---|---|
| **Network** | People who follow you, and followers of the publication it's in | Any story that breaks no rule. Undisclosed AI writing stays here |
| **General** | Readers matched by topic and by the writers and publications they follow | Medium's system decides; useful, clear stories in followed topics |
| **Boost** | Many more readers: home page, emails, apps | Picked by people (Medium's curators, about half nominated by publication editors) against the Boost guidelines |

Not distributed at all: sponsored content, content marketing, stories whose only purpose is signups or traffic, and
link farms. **A post about your product is content marketing.** A tutorial that solves a reader's problem, where your
product appears once as one honest option, is not.

## What that means for you
- **Publications are the shortcut.** A new account has no followers; a publication brings its own readers and its
  editors are the ones who nominate for Boost ([06](06-publications-and-boost.md)).
- **Topics matter.** Up to 5 per story. Pick the topics your readers follow, most specific first.
- **First lines matter.** Readers decide in the first screen; a read only counts for earnings after 30 seconds.
- **Fresh matters for Boost.** Stories older than 6 months can't be nominated, so the first weeks count.
- **Traffic from outside helps.** Reads from search, social and email earn more in the Partner Program; sharing your
  story on X, Reddit (where allowed) and your newsletter is expected, not cheating.

## What Medium doesn't publish
The exact ranking for General distribution and how curators weigh signals. Anything claiming a formula ("X claps in
the first hour = Boost") is a guess.
