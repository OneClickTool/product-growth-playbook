# 05 · Article playbook: 8 article types that work for makers

Rule one: **the article must be worth reading if your product didn't exist.** Medium doesn't distribute content
marketing and suspends accounts that post mainly to drive traffic to a product
([facts](../reference/medium-facts.md#rules-and-spam)). Your product earns its place as one honest option, a short
disclosed line at the end, and your bio.

## The 8 types

| # | Type | Title pattern | Product mention | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **How-to / tutorial** | "How to {{do X}} in {{tool/OS}} (without {{pain}})" | One step can use your tool, next to a free/manual way | Dev tools, Mac apps, extensions |
| 2 | **Fix a specific problem** | "{{Error / annoyance}}: why it happens and 3 ways to fix it" | Option 3 of 3, disclosed | Mac apps, extensions, plugins |
| 3 | **Honest comparison** | "{{A}} vs {{B}} vs {{C}} for {{use}}: what I'd pick" | One row in the table, disclosed; say where it loses | Any product with known rivals |
| 4 | **Build story with real numbers** | "I built {{product}} in {{time}}. Here's what {{N}} users taught me" | It's the subject, but the lessons are the value; real numbers only | SaaS, AI tools, indie makers |
| 5 | **Lesson / opinion** | "{{Common advice}} is wrong for {{audience}}" | Bio only | Building followers |
| 6 | **Deep dive on how it works** | "How {{technology}} actually works, explained with code" | "I used this in {{product}}" at the end | Dev tools, AI products |
| 7 | **Checklist / reference** | "The {{N}}-point checklist I use before {{task}}" | One item, or a link to a free template | Templates, B2B tools |
| 8 | **Case study** | "How {{a real user}} cut {{task}} from {{A}} to {{B}}" | Central, but only with the user's permission and real data | B2B, SaaS |

Types 1–3 bring readers from search for years. Type 4 and 5 bring followers. Start with 1–3.

## Shape of one article
1. **Title:** the reader's problem or result, specific. Subtitle: who it's for + what they'll get.
2. **First 3 lines:** the problem in their words, and the promise. No "In today's fast-paced world".
3. **Body:** short sections with headings, real steps, screenshots or code you made, numbers you measured.
   5–10 minutes of reading is a good default for tutorials.
4. **Product, at most once in the body,** as one option among others, with "I make this" in the same sentence.
5. **Last paragraph:** one sentence on who you are and one link with UTM:
   `?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=<article-slug>`.
6. **Topics:** up to 5, specific first (e.g. `macOS`, `Productivity`, `Mac`, `Apple`, `Software`).

## Product mention test (all must be yes)
- The article is useful with the product line deleted.
- The mention answers what the article is about, not a different problem.
- You say it's yours, in the same sentence.
- There's a free or manual alternative in the article too.
- It's one mention in the body, plus the last paragraph. Not five.

## AI and images
- Write it yourself. AI outlining and grammar checks are fine; a mostly-AI article can't be paywalled and gets less
  distribution ([facts](../reference/medium-facts.md#ai-content)). If AI helped substantially, say so near the top.
- Use your own screenshots; caption any AI image as AI. Never present an AI image as a real user or a real result.

## Paywall or free?
| Choose | When |
|---|---|
| **Paywalled** (Partner Program) | You want Medium income and the article's main goal is reach inside Medium. Share it with a [Friend Link](https://help.medium.com/hc/en-us/articles/360006543813-Friend-Links) on X, Reddit and email so non-members can read |
| **Free** | The article is your product's best tutorial and you'll link it from your README, docs and emails. Every visitor should read it to the end |
