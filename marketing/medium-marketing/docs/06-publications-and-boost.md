# 06 · Publications and Boost

A publication is a Medium magazine run by editors. For a new writer it's the fastest way to readers: the
publication's followers see your story, and its editors may nominate it for Boost
([facts](../reference/medium-facts.md#distribution-and-boost)).

## Find 3–5 publications
1. Read the top stories in your topics on Medium; note which publications they're in.
2. For each, check: posted in the last 2 weeks? clear submission page? accepts writers like you? topic fits?
   Publications move or close (Towards Data Science left Medium in 2025), so check before pitching.
3. Prefer a smaller, active publication in your exact niche over a huge general one that replies to nobody.
4. Log them in the [publication tracker](../templates/publication-tracker.md).

## Submit
1. Follow their submission page exactly (form, "add me as a writer", or email). Many want an unpublished draft.
2. Once you're a writer, submit the draft from the story's ⋯ menu → Add to publication.
3. One publication at a time per story. If they decline or don't answer within their stated time, withdraw and try
   the next.
4. Tell them if it's a republish with a canonical link.
5. Never pitch a product announcement. Pitch the reader's problem.

## Boost
- Boost is chosen by people: Medium's curators and publication editors in the Boost Nomination Program.
- You can't ask for it, and you can't buy it. What helps: a clear, specific, well-made story, your own experience,
  no promotion, no AI writing, published in an active publication in your topic.
- Only stories under 6 months old can be nominated. Publish, submit and share in the first weeks.

## Your own publication?
Later, maybe. A publication with your name ("Notes from {{Product}}") is a tidy archive, but it has no readers until
you bring them. Write for existing publications first.
