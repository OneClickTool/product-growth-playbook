# 01 · Strategy: is Medium worth it for your product?

**Short answer: yes as a support channel, rarely as the main one.** Medium is good at three things for a small
product: long, searchable how-to articles that keep being read; a ready-made reading audience of developers,
product people and founders; and a little side income from the Partner Program. It's weak at sending clicks:
outbound links are nofollow, many readers hit a paywall, and Medium suspends accounts whose stories exist mainly
to push traffic to a product ([facts](../reference/medium-facts.md#rules-and-spam)).

## Fit check

| Your product | Fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Dev tool, API, CLI, VS Code / JetBrains plugin | **Good** | Readers search "how to do X in Y"; a tutorial that uses your tool is the natural product mention |
| AI tool, automation, productivity SaaS, B2B | **Good** | Big reading topics on Medium (AI, productivity, startups); case studies and "how I automated…" work |
| Mac app, browser extension, Notion template | **Medium** | Works with "how to fix X on Mac / in Chrome" tutorials; the buyers are mostly on Reddit, X and the stores |
| Mobile app for consumers, casual game | **Weak** | Players don't read Medium; use [TikTok](../../tiktok-marketing/README-TikTok-Marketing.md) or [Instagram](../../instagram-marketing/README-Instagram-Marketing.md) |
| You already have a blog with traffic | **Good as a mirror** | Republish there with a canonical link: more readers, no SEO cost ([04](04-canonical-and-republishing.md)) |
| You want Medium to earn money by itself | **Weak** | Earnings come only from paying members' reading time; plan for side income, not a salary |

## What Medium does for you, and what it doesn't

| Medium gives you | Medium doesn't give you |
|---|---|
| Readers who follow topics and publications, before you have an audience | Search ranking for your own site (links are nofollow) |
| Articles that Google can find, on a strong domain | Many clicks: a story with 5,000 reads may send a few dozen visits |
| Credibility: a good tutorial is something you can link in sales emails, READMEs and X posts | A place to post ads or launch announcements (they get no distribution) |
| Partner Program income (Vietnam is a supported country) | Control: Medium changes distribution and pay rules often |

## The plan: three phases

| Phase | Weeks | Do | Don't | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Set up and mirror** | 1 | Profile ([02](02-profile-setup.md)); import 2–3 of your best existing posts with canonical links ([04](04-canonical-and-republishing.md)) | Copy-paste without a canonical link | Your profile has 3 real articles and a link to your site |
| **2 · Publish weekly** | 2–8 | One useful article a week ([05](05-article-playbook.md)), written for the reader; product only where it is the honest answer, at the end | Write about your product; publish AI drafts unedited | 6–8 articles, 1–3 accepted by publications ([06](06-publications-and-boost.md)) |
| **3 · Keep what works** | 9+ | Read the [numbers](07-metrics-and-stop-rules.md): keep the article types that bring followers and site visits; drop the rest | Chase views that send no one to your site | A repeatable article type, or a clear "stop" |

## How Medium fits the other channels
Medium is the long version of a fact you already posted short. One true fact → an X post or Reddit answer this week
→ a Medium tutorial that explains it fully → that tutorial linked from your README, landing FAQ and emails.
Hub: [Marketing & Social](../../README-marketing-social.md).

```text
Medium article (useful by itself)  →  last paragraph + bio  →  your site / free trial  (utm_source=medium)
                                   ↘  followers + publication readers  →  next article
```
